# Singapore Tenancy Agreement Review

## Purpose and scope

Apply an experienced Singapore leasing lawyer's approach: establish whether the occupier can lawfully use and retain the premises, understand the full financial commitment, and test default and exit consequences. This workflow supports legal analysis; it does not confer professional status or guarantee a lawyer-equivalent result.

Establish whether the client is landlord, tenant, subtenant or guarantor. If unspecified, show both principal perspectives. Classify the arrangement as residential/private, HDB, commercial office/industrial, qualifying retail, or potentially a licence. Examine substance and the premises, not merely the agreement's title.

## Intake and method

Record the review date, premises, use, parties, lease/renewal dates, rent, all additional charges, deposit, guarantees, fit-out budget and proposed occupation date. Read the letter of offer, main lease, house rules, inventory, condition photographs, fit-out manual, guarantees, side letters and incorporated documents together. Identify missing material and conflicts.

Walk through handover, fit-out, occupation, rent review, renewal, default and reinstatement. Stress-test approval refusal, late handover, damage/unusable premises, landlord insolvency, sale of the building, premature exit and disputed deposit deductions. Quantify total committed cost and plausible exit exposure from available facts. Distinguish absent evidence from an established breach.

## Mandatory authority discipline

- Every substantive risk finding must cite a relevant Singapore statute or judgment, with a primary-source link and section/subsection or judgment paragraph, followed by an explanation of its application. A list of statutes at the end does not satisfy this requirement.
- Read the actual authority and verify the applicable version, commencement, exceptions, transitional rules and later judicial treatment. Do not mistake counsel's submissions for the court's holding.
- Distinguish mandatory protections, enforceability uncertainty and commercial preferences. For a negotiable protection, explain the legal consequence of its absence without claiming the protection is compulsory.
- If an authority cannot be verified, put the concern in **Unverified concerns / research required**, state conditional impact and identify the research needed. Never manufacture a case, paragraph or statutory requirement to fill a citation column.
- Regulatory guidance and the retail Code may supplement statutory analysis. Explain their legal status and cite the enabling Act where applicable. Foreign tenancy rules and foreign cases are not Singapore law.
- Sources below are starting authorities selected on 21 September 2026, not a certification of the law for a later lease. Recheck them during each review and disclose unavailable sources.

## Severity

| Severity | Test | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Credible inability to lawfully occupy/use the premises, loss of the central bargain, or catastrophic exposure without a realistic remedy | Resolve the identified blocker before commitment/occupation |
| High | Material eviction, business interruption, unbounded cost, security or recovery exposure under a realistic scenario | Amend or obtain explicit commercial acceptance |
| Medium | Manageable cost, repair, administration or exit uncertainty | Negotiate a proportionate protection |
| Low | Limited consequence or minor clarification | Address after material issues |

Assess impact, likelihood, timing, reversibility and recoverability. Explain each rating and state legal confidence separately. A large deposit is not automatically illegal; a missing diplomatic clause is not automatically High. A tenant and landlord may reasonably rate the same clause differently. Give conditional ratings where key facts are unknown.

## Main review areas

### 1. Authority, premises and permitted use

Check ownership/authority to let, headlease limits, required mortgagee/headlandlord consent, exact demise, shared areas, access, parking and service rights. Investigate whether the intended activity is permitted and whether approvals are obtainable in time. Treat approval responsibility, cost, deadline and consequences of refusal as separate drafting issues.

For HDB/private residential occupation or industrial premises, verify the relevant eligibility, rental/use conditions and competent authority's current requirements; do not import one category's rules into another. Support any asserted statutory restriction with the actual enabling legislation and provision.

[Civil Law Act 1909, s 6(d)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLA1909?ProvIds=pr6-) is a starting point on signed written evidence for dispositions of land interests. Research applicable formalities and exceptions before alleging invalidity.

For use-approval disputes, investigate [Sheng Siong Supermarket Pte Ltd v Carilla Pte Ltd [2011] SGHC 204](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2011_SGHC_204). It addresses a lease and intended-use difficulties; read the relevant reasoning and identify exact paragraphs before relying on it. Do not infer that every refusal of approval automatically releases a tenant.

### 2. Retail statutory gateway

Check [Lease Agreements for Retail Premises Act 2023, s 2, ss 5–9 and First/Second Schedules](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/LARPA2023). Establish whether the premises, agreement date and term satisfy the qualifying-lease regime. The original framework uses a one-year threshold and disregards optional extensions when testing a particular term; verify the applicable version and assess renewals separately.

For a qualifying lease, test compliance with the applicable Code leasing principles, permitted deviations and required declarations. Read the Code version governing signing, not simply the latest available copy. Examine exclusivity, costs, advertising/promotions, pre-termination, security, floor-area changes and other applicable principles. Cite the precise Code paragraph alongside the Act. Do not treat all deviations as permissible merely because both parties sign.

Use [FTIC guidance](https://www.ftic.org.sg/faq/) to locate Code and process details, and verify statutory complaint procedures/time limits. Do not apply the retail regime indiscriminately to homes, offices or all commercial premises.

### 3. Rent, deposit and guarantees

Model base rent, turnover rent, service charges, taxes/GST where applicable, escalation, rent review, utilities, insurance and administration costs. Check audit rights, unilateral increases, double charging and rent-free-period clawbacks. Identify whether the quoted figure is the full occupancy cost.

Review deposit custody, authorised deductions, evidence, replenishment, return deadline, disputes and transfer on sale. Examine bank-guarantee demand wording, expiry and release. For personal guarantees, identify scope, cap, duration, variations and release; check [Civil Law Act 1909, s 6(b)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLA1909?ProvIds=pr6-) when signed evidence of a guarantee is material. Do not claim a universal Singapore deposit cap or mandatory escrow scheme.

### 4. Repairs, condition, indemnities and interruption

Allocate structural repairs, latent/pre-existing defects, routine maintenance, air-conditioning, statutory upgrades and common-area systems. Match repair and reinstatement promises against the inventory, condition schedule, fair-wear provisions and actual control of the premises.

Test indemnities, negligence exclusions, insurance, deductibles and double recovery. Before relying on [Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, ss 1–3, 11 and First Schedule](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/UCTA1977), establish scope: land-interest transactions engage important exclusions. Do not assume reasonableness review applies to every lease term.

Check access notice, emergencies, quiet enjoyment, interruption of utilities, casualty, rent suspension and termination if the premises remain unusable. Obtain issue-specific Singapore authority for implied obligations; do not invent a general statutory right to withhold rent or repair-and-deduct.

### 5. Default, forfeiture and exit

Review breach triggers, notice/service, cure, re-entry, distress, acceleration of future rent, deposit forfeiture and recovery costs. Distinguish termination rights, possession remedies and damages.

- [Conveyancing and Law of Property Act 1886, s 18](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLPA1886?ProvIds=pr18-): examine statutory notice/remedy requirements and relief, including exclusions. Do not assume the same process applies to every breach.
- [Section 18A](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLPA1886?ProvIds=pr18A-): separately investigate relief in actions for non-payment of rent. Do not state that contractual wording authorises unrestricted lockouts or disposals of possessions.
- [Denka Advantech Pte Ltd and another v Seraya Energy Pte Ltd and another and other appeals [2020] SGCA 119, [185]](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2020_SGCA_119): investigate breach-triggered fixed sums under Singapore's penalty doctrine. Distinguish compensation, genuine security, forfeiture and primary obligations before drawing conclusions.
- [RDC Concrete Pte Ltd v Sato Kogyo (S) Pte Ltd and another appeal [2007] SGCA 39, [90]–[113]](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2007_SGCA_39): analyse contractual/common-law termination; not every breach justifies ending the lease. Account for lease-specific law.

Check break/diplomatic clauses, assignment/subletting, change of control, redevelopment/relocation, renewal deadlines, holdover, reinstatement and release of guarantees. Treat a requested break clause or compensation formula as a negotiated protection unless a verified applicable rule says otherwise.

### 6. Stamping and dispute route

Check allocation and timing under [Stamp Duties Act 1929, ss 34, 42–47 and 52](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SDA1929). Distinguish evidential consequences and available cure from automatic invalidity. Use [IRAS renting guidance](https://www.iras.gov.sg/taxes/stamp-duty/for-property/renting-a-property) for current practical details, supported by the applicable law.

Check governing law, service addresses, jurisdiction, mediation/arbitration and enforcement practicality. Verify any Small Claims Tribunals eligibility or retail statutory route against current jurisdictional provisions and deadlines; do not assume every lease dispute qualifies.

## Required output

Start with client perspective, lease category, document version, review/research date, missing documents and the three to five most important risks. Give a conditional recommendation: **proceed / proceed subject to changes / resolve blockers first**.

| Rank / ID | Severity / confidence | Clause / page or omission | Consequence and affected party | Singapore authority / pinpoint | Proposed fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For each issue, include a short clause quotation, realistic trigger scenario, amount/impact where calculable, rating rationale, legal rule and application, counterargument/uncertainty, replacement wording or concrete amendment, fallback and residual risk. Identify who must supply an approval, document or decision and by when, if known.

End with outstanding approval/document requests, signing and handover conditions, unverified concerns and sources with access/version dates. Check that every substantive ranked finding has supporting authority; flag negotiable terms honestly; and ensure fixes align across default, deposit, indemnity and exit provisions.
