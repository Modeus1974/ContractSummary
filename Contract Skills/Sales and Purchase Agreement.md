# Singapore Sales and Purchase Agreement Review

## Purpose and scope

Apply the analytical discipline of an experienced Singapore transactional lawyer: identify what could defeat the transaction, destroy its economic value or prevent recovery, then propose workable protections. This is a review workflow, not a representation that the reviewer is a practising lawyer or a substitute for transaction-specific professional judgment.

Review from the instructed buyer's or seller's perspective. If unspecified, show both perspectives without silently choosing one. Identify the subject matter before applying law: **Singapore real estate, shares, a business/asset transfer, or goods**. Do not apply goods legislation to land or shares. For mixed transactions, analyse each component separately.

## Intake and review method

1. Record the review date, parties and capacity, governing law, transaction type, value, deposit, signing/completion dates, financing, commercial objective and negotiating constraints.
2. Read the agreement as a whole, including definitions, schedules, option, disclosure letter, incorporated conditions, guarantees and side letters. Reconcile conflicting documents and priority clauses. Identify missing documents without inventing their contents.
3. Map the sequence from signing through payment, transfer, completion and post-completion claims. Test failure scenarios: missing approval, defective title, financing failure, delayed completion, undisclosed liability, insolvency and failed enforcement.
4. Distinguish a drafting ambiguity, a commercial allocation of risk, a due-diligence gap and a potential legal defect. Quantify exposure using supplied figures; otherwise describe the scale and the missing inputs.
5. Focus first on material issues. Merge duplicate symptoms of the same problem, but explain interacting provisions such as a warranty defeated by an exclusion and a short claim period.

## Mandatory authority discipline

- Every substantive risk finding must cite at least one **relevant Singapore statute or Singapore judgment**, with a working primary-source link and section/subsection or judgment paragraph. A bibliography alone is insufficient.
- Read the supporting provision or judgment passage before relying on it. Confirm the applicable statutory version, commencement, exceptions and transitional rules for the relevant transaction date. Check later treatment of cases and distinguish appellate holdings from first-instance reasoning, submissions and dicta.
- Explain the legal proposition and its connection to this clause. Do not use a generic citation as decoration or assert that the law mandates a negotiated protection such as an escrow, basket or cap.
- Commercial concerns may rely on a verified legal principle explaining the consequences of the bargain, while expressly identifying the proposed protection as negotiable. If no supporting authority can be verified, place the point under **Unverified concerns / research required**, with conditional impact; do not present it as an established legal defect or fabricate authority.
- Prefer Singapore Statutes Online and Singapore Courts judgments. Regulatory guidance and industry conditions supplement, but do not replace, the required statute/case reference. Identify foreign authorities as persuasive only.
- The authorities below are research starting points, selected on 21 September 2026. They are not an exhaustive or permanently current statement of law. Recheck them on every review; disclose inaccessible sources and incomplete research.

## Severity and prioritisation

| Severity | Decision rule | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Critical | Credible illegality, inability to transfer the essential asset, or potentially catastrophic/irrecoverable exposure that defeats the transaction | Resolve before signing/completion; identify the precise condition or evidence needed |
| High | Material financial exposure, loss of a core protection, or serious enforcement difficulty under a realistic scenario | Seek amendment or express commercial acceptance before commitment |
| Medium | Meaningful but manageable cost, uncertainty or operational friction | Negotiate a proportionate fix or mitigation |
| Low | Limited consequence or administrative ambiguity | Clarify if worthwhile; avoid distracting from material issues |

Rank by impact, plausible likelihood, timing, reversibility and ability to recover. Explain the rating in one sentence; do not mechanically label every missing clause High. A mandatory-law issue with a minor remediable consequence need not be Critical. State legal confidence separately as high, medium or low, and identify disputed facts. Where exposure depends on missing facts, give a conditional rating and the fact that changes it.

## Transaction-specific review

### Real estate

- Verify the property description, estate/tenure, registered owner, encumbrances, caveats, occupiers and authority to sell. Require evidence of discharge and registrable transfer, rather than assuming contractual warranties establish title.
- Check eligibility and approvals, including foreign ownership restrictions, HDB requirements or developer-sale rules where applicable. Research the relevant provisions before making an illegality finding.
- Examine option exercise, deposit stakeholder/release arrangements, financing assumptions, conditions precedent, long-stop date, completion deliverables, vacant possession, defects and remedies for failed completion.
- Allocate duty, tax, outgoings and apportionments; verify rates and deadlines at the relevant date. Identify the edition and incorporation of any Law Society conditions; do not assume they apply automatically.
- Research anchors: [Civil Law Act 1909, s 6(d)](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLA1909?ProvIds=pr6-) on signed written evidence for land dispositions; [Conveyancing and Law of Property Act 1886](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CLPA1886) for applicable conveyancing rules; [Stamp Duties Act 1929, ss 34, 42–47 and 52](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SDA1929) for liability, stamping and evidential consequences. Check exceptions and cure mechanisms; do not equate an unstamped instrument with automatic invalidity.

### Shares and business assets

- Distinguish acquisition of a company with its existing liabilities from selected assets and assumed liabilities. Test the definition of the acquired perimeter, excluded assets, debt, cash and working capital.
- Check ownership, transfer restrictions, approvals, constitutional requirements, pre-emption rights, security releases and completion registration. For a substantial disposal, investigate [Companies Act 1967, s 160](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/CoA1967?ProvIds=pr160-); it is not a universal approval rule for every SPA.
- Review pricing adjustments, locked-box leakage, earn-outs, accounting policies, independent determination, escrow and set-off. Model incentives for manipulation and disputes.
- Test warranties, disclosure standards, knowledge qualifiers, tax covenants, specific indemnities, caps, baskets, survival periods and claim-notice requirements together. Check collectability, not merely the nominal remedy.
- Identify required novations, landlord/licensor consents, employee-transfer consequences and regulatory clearances. Research the applicable employment, competition, data and sector-specific legislation for the actual business.

### Goods

- Review specifications, inspection/testing, acceptance, delivery, title, risk, insurance, retention of title, rejection, replacement and warranty support. Align delivery terminology with the version actually incorporated.
- Use [Sale of Goods Act 1979, ss 12–15, 16–20, 34–35 and 51–53](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/SGA1979) for title/quality terms, property and risk, acceptance and remedies. Check the effect of s 15A in non-consumer cases and contractual variation.
- Test exclusions under [Unfair Contract Terms Act 1977, ss 2–3, 6, 11, 26 and First Schedule](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/UCTA1977), including scope and international-supply exclusions. Do not assume every negotiated commercial contract attracts the same controls. Check consumer and international-sales regimes when triggered by the facts.

## Cross-cutting legal issues

| Issue | Authority and application |
|---|---|
| Non-reliance, entire agreement and misstatements | [Misrepresentation Act 1967, ss 2–3](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/MA1967?ProvIds=pr2-,pr3-): assess remedies and reasonableness of exclusions. Distinguish contractual warranties from pre-contract representations. |
| Default, termination and failed conditions | [RDC Concrete Pte Ltd v Sato Kogyo (S) Pte Ltd and another appeal [2007] SGCA 39, [90]–[113]](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2007_SGCA_39): distinguish express termination rights, renunciation, conditions and sufficiently serious consequences. Do not assume every breach permits termination. |
| Fixed compensation, default interest and forfeiture | [Denka Advantech Pte Ltd and another v Seraya Energy Pte Ltd and another and other appeals [2020] SGCA 119, [185]](https://www.elitigation.sg/gd/s/2020_SGCA_119): the penalty rule concerns breach-triggered obligations and retains the Dunlop approach. Analyse substance; do not automatically apply it to genuine deposits or primary payment obligations. Research the separate deposit doctrine when needed. |

Also assess governing law/forum consistency, service of notices, guarantor capacity, third-party rights, assignment, limitation periods and enforcement where assets are located. Retrieve issue-specific authority rather than treating these starting cases as universal support.

## Required review output

Start with the reviewed version, client perspective, legal research date, documents missing and a provisional recommendation: **proceed / proceed subject to changes / resolve blockers first**. Identify the three to five largest risks, or fewer if warranted.

Use a severity-sorted register:

| Rank / ID | Severity and confidence | Clause / page or omission | Risk and affected party | Singapore authority and pinpoint | Recommended action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

For each finding, provide:

1. A short quotation or precise description of the clause and relevant cross-references.
2. The failure scenario, monetary/operational consequence and reason for the rating.
3. The verified rule, its application, limitations and any credible counterargument.
4. Proposed replacement wording or a concrete amendment, a negotiation fallback and residual risk. Label commercial positions as such; avoid unsupported numerical caps or time periods.
5. Outstanding evidence or decision, responsible party and deadline where known.

Finish with conditions to satisfy before signing/completion, document requests, unverified concerns and an authority list with version/access dates. Do not give an unqualified clearance where material documents or authority remain unavailable.

## Final quality check

Ensure each ranked legal finding has a relevant pinpoint citation and factual basis; ratings reflect the client's actual exposure; clauses interact coherently; proposed wording does not introduce a new inconsistency; and hypothetical risks are not reported as discovered facts. Never imply that missing financing, searches or consents have been verified from the agreement alone.
